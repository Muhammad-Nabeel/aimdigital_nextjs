SEO and AI-search optimisation both benefit from pages that are accessible, useful and clear about their subject. Traditional search presents links, while answer engines may synthesise responses from selected sources. Build a sound website and answer real customer questions first, then evaluate discoverability without treating any format or file as a guarantee of inclusion.

## What does AI-search optimisation mean?

**AI-search optimisation** describes work intended to make information easier for answer engines to discover, interpret and use. The term is also discussed as generative engine optimisation (GEO) or answer engine optimisation (AEO). It does not describe a switch that ensures a business will appear in an answer.

The practical starting point is content that directly answers a question and supports the answer with appropriate detail. Readers should be able to see who wrote it, what it applies to and where its evidence comes from.

## Which foundations do SEO and AI search share?

Start with pages people can use and crawlers can reach. Give each page a clear subject, descriptive headings and useful internal links. Address confusing navigation, accidental indexing restrictions and incomplete content before adding another layer of optimisation.

| Focus           | Traditional search                  | AI answer tools                              |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Useful content  | Helps a visitor evaluate a result   | Provides information an answer may draw from |
| Clear structure | Explains the subject of a page      | Makes specific answers easier to interpret   |
| Evidence        | Supports credibility for the reader | Helps establish what a claim is based on     |

### What should an answer-first page look like?

Begin with a concise explanation of the main question. Follow it with definitions, scope, examples and limitations. Use a comparison table when readers need to assess alternatives, rather than adding tables simply to imitate an optimisation template.

For a service page, explain the outcome, included work, deliverables and how pricing is agreed. Avoid vague promises that cannot be checked against a proposal.

## What should you measure?

Use available search and website data to observe indexing, queries, visits and useful actions. For answer tools, keep a dated record of observed mentions and the questions tested. A single response is a snapshot rather than proof of consistent visibility.

Review changes with an agreed goal. More impressions are not automatically more qualified enquiries, and a citation does not automatically imply an endorsement.

## How can you turn the idea into a content plan?

Group related customer questions into a topic and decide which page answers each one. Link supporting guides to the relevant service and back to a clear overview. Give existing pages a chance to do their job before creating overlapping content.
