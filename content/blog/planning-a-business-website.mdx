A useful website plan defines the audience, the action you want visitors to take and the information they need to decide. Start with business goals, then agree content, features and responsibilities. This helps your team choose an appropriate scope, identify dependencies and evaluate the finished website against needs rather than appearance alone.

## Who is the website for?

Write down the people who will use the website and the questions they bring. A prospective customer may need a clear explanation of your service, examples of relevant work and a straightforward way to enquire. An existing customer may need documentation, order information or support.

A **visitor journey** is the sequence of steps someone takes to reach a useful outcome. Sketch the shortest sensible route from the entry page to that outcome. Use that route to decide what information belongs on each page.

## What should a website brief include?

Your brief should explain the offer, audience and main action. Include the current website if one exists, the pages you expect, necessary integrations, content ownership and practical constraints. Distinguish essential requirements from ideas that can be considered later.

| Requirement   | Question to answer                                      |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audience      | Who needs the website and what do they need to know?    |
| Content       | Who supplies, checks and approves the words and images? |
| Functionality | Which features are essential for the first launch?      |
| Operations    | Who will maintain the site and respond to enquiries?    |

### How should you approach integrations?

List the systems that must connect to the website, such as payments, inventory or scheduling. Access to an API does not automatically mean every desired workflow is possible. Review documentation, permissions and ownership before relying on an integration in the launch plan.

## How do you choose a realistic scope?

Start with the smallest scope that supports the agreed visitor journey. Discuss design, development, content, review and testing together. A missing product catalogue or approval process can affect timing just as much as unfinished code.

Ask the project team to explain assumptions and dependencies in its proposal. Changes may be useful, but their impact on cost and milestones should be discussed before additional work starts.

## What should be checked before launch?

Review the main journeys on relevant devices, keyboard navigation, form behaviour, links and error states. Confirm who controls the domain and hosting, who receives enquiries and how future updates will be handled. Agree the checks appropriate to the project instead of assuming a single checklist covers every business.
